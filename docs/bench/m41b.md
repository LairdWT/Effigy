# M41b: the limb rule and the shark

`python -m tools.creature_bench fox horse bird kaiju shark dragon --glb <cat>=fox --glb <test kaiju>=kaiju --glb
<test shark>=shark --glb <test dragon>=dragon` (Mesh2Motion's pinned sample meshes, four turns each; `029_rig_fit`
is the owner's cat on the fox template; the `test` rows are copies of the owner's `test kaiju`, `test shark`,
`test dragon` `005_clean`, in that order). Before = M41a (6927898), after = M41b. Inside is after rig_fit's snap,
score is Check fit's.

The rule (`steps/_templates.py`, `_thin` + `_limb`, replacing M36's `_tail`): every chain that sticks out of the
mesh (the tail by name, and each limb from the trunk while a bone has one child: fingers and toes hang off its last
bone) is laid from its last joint inside along the mesh's own thin part (the opening by a ball a third of the body's
depth), its joints at the template's bone-length fractions; what hangs off a moved bone turns and stretches with it.
The thin part: not taken by another chain, within a quarter of the size of the start, at least a quarter of the
chain long, nearest the placed chain, on the chain's own side of the middle plane for a sided chain, wholly above the
floor unless the chain stands on the template's floor. A trace is kept only when more of the joints it moves are
inside after rig_fit's snap (a tie: more inside before it). A template hanging below its root (the shark) floats: no
feet to map, its root goes with the hips, no floor limit.
