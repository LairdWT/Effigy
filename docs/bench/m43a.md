# M43a: cheap wins in `bake` (supersample 2x, AO into ORM R, UV seam levelling)

RTX 5080 Laptop, 2048 px, the step run on the library's `uv` versions (read only, output to a temp dir):
pirate = `bench_faithful_pirate/080_uv`, chest = `bench_faithful_chest/060_uv`, cat = `cat/025_uv`.
`old` = `supersample 1, ao false` (the previous bake; the seam levelling is always on, `seam before` is the old figure).
`new` = defaults (`supersample 2, ao true`). `peak VRAM` = torch peak allocated inside the step.

## Findings (what changed against the plan)

- **AO rays go against the version's own mesh, not the hi mesh.** First try (rays from the hi-surface point, hi BVH, as
  planned) gave whole UV charts of the pirate's sleeves and the cat's back black: the hi mesh is a double shell with
  interior layers. Second try (low mesh BVH) still blotched the sleeves: ~9 % of the pirate's texels sat on faces that
  still face inward (`ao(n) < 0.15` while `ao(-n) > 0.85`). The step now turns the AO normal outward per face with
  `steps/_mesh.inward_faces` (the ray-vote of `face_outward`, split out; `face_outward` calls it, nothing else changes):
  inward-looking texels 9.0 % -> 0.1 %, no blotches in the renders.
- `ao_mean` of the pirate (0.54) is low because the low mesh keeps hidden layers (the arm inside the sleeve, a lining):
  they are dark and invisible. Visible surfaces look right; the renders show crevices (under the arms, hat brim, belt)
  darker, nothing else.
- The seam levelling is a linear fade of the shift to the two-sided mean over 2 texels (not a hard step at 2 px).
- The chest shows the least AO (0.96): a convex box with thin trim; the effect there is the corners and the lock.
