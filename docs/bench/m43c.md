47d0d7a with the `m43c` working tree. The library's `uv` versions re-baked with the current `bake` (defaults:

## What the numbers say

- **The metric falls as the amount rises.** The lantern (brass creases, the globe's rim) goes from 0.385 to 0.265
  at 0.5 and 0.072 at 1.0; the pirate (little shadow in its colour to begin with: 0.107, Faithful bakes the
  image's own even light) to 0.046. The chest's colour holds no shadow that follows its AO (−0.034: a convex box
  with thin trim) and the fit's slope is negative, so nothing is divided and its texture stays byte for byte.
- **Cost**: ~2 s and 1.5 GB (the coverage raster at 2×; the delight itself is per-texel arithmetic), no model.
  A delight-only version carries no `views.png`.
- **In the renders** the difference is subtle, as it should be: the crevices (under the lantern's cap, the
  pirate's sleeves against the vest) carry one shadow instead of two; nothing else moves. The owner picks the
  default from the sheet (0 / 0.5 / 1); 0 stays until then.

## Found on the way (and fixed)

- **Every `refine` rewrote bake's edge texels.** Its closing pass (seam levelling, gutter fill) used the
  texel-centre coverage of a 1× raster, while bake's coverage is the 2× raster's "any quarter covered", one
  ring of texels wider. `fill_gutters` then overwrote that ring with push-pull values, and the seam samples sit
  on it: on an *unchanged* texture the chest's seam delta went 2.285 → 1.591 after `heal_seams` and → 6.202
  after `fill_gutters` (the pirate 1.187 → 1.015 → 2.806). M43b's bench had attributed its rising seam deltas to
  the views. `refine` now rasterizes bake's mask (2×, any quarter; 1× at 4096) for those passes: the chest
  2.285 → 1.163.
- **The divide widens a seam where the two sides' AO differ** (two texel positions for one 3D point, 32-ray
  noise), by up to the fit's 4× in crevices. The AO channel's seams are now levelled with `heal_seams` before
  dividing: on the same bakes the lantern's seam delta at 1.0 went 8.424 → 7.184 and the pirate's 2.558 → 2.373
  (the 5.705 above is on the final bake). The rest is the amplification of the colour's own seam difference and
  stays in the base colour only; an engine multiplies the AO back in, which undoes it.

## The pirate's black triangles, fixed in bake (branch `ao-enclosed`)

The sheet above shows black triangles on the pirate's shirt, face and trousers wherever the AO is applied; without
the AO the texture is clean. 24.5 % of the pirate's bright (luminance > 180) texels had AO < 0.1. Replaying
bake's rays on the mesh in the library frame: of the 1 951 faces a ray reaches from the sheet's camera, 24 are
such holes, and for those the ray along the normal hits at 6–30 mm (median 12 mm), the ray against it at
0.2–2.5 mm (median 0.7 mm); the hemisphere is open within 5 mm (openness 1.0) and closed within 2 cm (0.33)
and 13 cm (0.06); a 20° cone toward the camera escapes 70 % (fine faces: 100 %). So they are faces of the
inner shell (the arm inside the sleeve, the inner wall of the head) seen where the outer shell is torn or open,
in a thin gap, and the AO is right to call them dark; the colour hides them because it is sampled from the
volume, which holds the shirt's colour there too. No UV overlap (0 contested texels), no mirrored frame (det
0.75, uniform), winding and vertex normals agree on every face.

What did not fix them (each measured on the bright-texel share at AO < 0.1, 24.5 % before): an orientation
vote by the openness on both sides of the vertex normal, with the AO's reach (19.3 %, by flipping enclosed
faces on a wrong criterion: the inside of a torso looks open within 0.1 × diag) and with an unlimited one
(24.6 %); marking faces no ray leaves either way as enclosed, AO 1 (5.0 %: the hidden inner shell, 34 % of the
texels, but the visible holes let a few rays out through the tear); lifting a face to the AO of the face its
normal ray hits within 5 cm, chained (4.6 %: what they look at is as dark); lifting a face more than 0.3 below
the median of its edge neighbours (4.7 %: their neighbours run on into the hidden layer). The visible holes are
not lone faces in mesh terms.

What fixed them: in the AO *map*. A hemisphere integral is smooth along one surface; a tiny island of
near-zero AO inside lit surface is another surface. `_bake.fill_dark_islands`: connected patches of texels below
0.25, smaller than 0.0003 of the atlas (1 258 texels at 2048², about seven faces of this mesh) and more than
0.3 below the median of the two-texel covered ring around them, take that median; a crevice is an extended
dark region and stays (CPU test: a 4 × 220 band keeps, a 4 × 4 island fills, a dark spot inside a dark region
keeps). With it, and the enclosed faces at AO 1 (`_bake.ao_faces`, which also replaces `inward_faces`' two-ray
orientation vote by the 64-ray escape share on both sides):
