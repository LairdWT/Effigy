# M23: faces from the image (2026-10-06)

**Accepted by the owner on 2026-10-06** ("good for now").

The owner's request after the M22 clips: "the models have big trouble with faces; make them look like the
generated images". The bake samples a 1024 volume, which gives a face about 60 voxels across: the nurse's
baked face was a smear with black eye holes while her conditioning image has a readable face at about
150 px. The new step `project` (IMPLEMENTATION M23) projects that image onto the base colour where the
generator's camera sees the surface, on the head by default.

## The premise, checked first

The Faithful generator (Pixal3D) is pixel-aligned with its conditioning image. Its camera, read from
`steps/trellis2.py` and the generator's `inference.py`: perspective, `fov` 0.2 rad, at `0.5 / tan(fov / 2)`
on +Z of the source frame (glTF axes, unit cube), NDC y up. Projecting the hi mesh's vertices with it onto
the image and comparing with the matte, on four bench assets:

| asset | silhouette bounding boxes, mesh vs matte (px, rows / cols) |
|---|---|
| nurse | 40–948 / 142–843 vs 36–950 / 141–844 |
| knight | 38–945 / 155–829 vs 37–949 / 153–832 |
| viking | 38–894 / 105–824 vs 34–897 / 104–827 |
| kid | 39–954 / 138–853 vs 36–955 / 134–858 |

Within 4 px everywhere; the y-flipped and mirrored conventions scored far worse. The Fast generator has
no such camera (DINOv3 conditioning) and no facing convention: the step skips such versions with a note.
A fitted orthographic camera was tried and dropped on review: a silhouette cannot tell a face from the
back of a head, so the gate could not have caught a mirrored fit.

**Thresholds** (set by eye on the nurse, then held across the 17): silhouette gate 0.8 (the Faithful
runs sit at 0.92–0.97, a wrong image or a moved frame falls far below); view-angle ramp smoothstep over
cos 0.25 → 0.6; head band: the chin at 55 % of the way from the nose to the shoulders, zero weight at the
shoulders, the nose assumed 40 % down from the top when unread, fallback band 12–18 % of the figure's
height; colour match needs 1000 texels under the image; z-test slop 1 % of the depth extent over the
farthest of the 3×3 neighbouring pixels.

## What was fixed along the way

- **Pale triangular blotches** over the nurse's face on the first run: the bake mesh has inward-wound
  patches (trimesh `is_winding_consistent` false) whose normals point into the head, so the cosine weight
  dropped them. The weight now uses smooth normals rebuilt from face normals turned towards the camera
  first: a texel that passed the z-test is the front surface whichever way its triangle is wound. Face
  normals alone (the first fix) faceted the blend on curved hair and caps.
- The z-test compares against the farthest depth among the pixel's 3×3 neighbours plus 1 % of the mesh's
  depth extent, so texels on triangle edges are not cut by the pixel grid (thin dark lines along edges
  in the first run).

## Does the rig fit still read the facing?

`rig_fit` renders the nearest textured ancestor for its pose detection, which is now the `project` version
(review finding). Run on four projected versions, against the earlier automatic fits:

| asset | facing by | face scores front / back | inside |
|---|---|---|---|
| dwarf (turnaround) | feet (as before) | 0.34 / 0.50 (the helmet; borderline, as before) | 52 |
| runner | default (as before) | 0.27 / 0.45 | 52 |
| viking | face (as before) | 0.80 / 0.55 (was 0.73 / 0.55) | 52 |
| kid | face | 0.90 / 0.62 | 52 |

No facing changed; the viking's face reads better. The projected face sits where the detector expects a
face, so this is the direction it should move.

## Open

- The image is lit, the albedo is not: the match moves only the mean colour (a fitted gain, tried first,
  flattened the faces to the smeared bake's contrast: gains 0.5–0.8 on the nurse), so the image's shading
  is baked into the albedo. A delighting pass (research 04 §4b, Marigold-style) would remove it;
  unscheduled.
- The image's face is about 150 px tall; an upscaler before projection would sharpen further.
- One view only: the sides and back of the head keep the bake. A second image (the turnaround's back
  view) could feed the same step.
- Facets on curved surfaces under the lit image (the kid's hair, the farmer's cap): inherent in projecting
  a lit image onto low-poly geometry; smaller with the smooth normals, gone only with delighting.

# M23b: heads and hands geometry (2026-10-06, investigation)

**Owner decision 2026-10-07: accepted as is for this iteration; the options below are noted for a version 2** (PLAN Phase 4, HANDOVER §9).

The owner's two geometry complaints from the M22 clips: the kid's torn hair at the back of the head and
the hiker's stub fingers. Each stage rendered (`m23b_stages.jpg`: generate `hi.glb`, clean, remesh, bake):

- **The kid's hair.** The generator makes hair as separate thin strands with gaps. `clean` has to rebuild
  the surface (the raw mesh is non-manifold: 87k shared edges) by narrow-band dual contouring at 512; the
  strands merge into a hood with a few pits where gaps were wider than a voxel (199 open edges after
  clean). `remesh` to 15k faces closes the mesh but widens the pits into dents; `bake` samples the volume
  at the dent, where it finds the scalp under the hair, so the dent shows a light patch, not black. A
  finer rebuild makes it worse, not better: at 1024 (`m23b_rebuild.jpg`, `310_clean` / `320_remesh`) the
  strands stay separate, 1263 open edges and 46 components after clean, large holes after remesh. The
  cure would be a *closing* of the narrow band before contouring (gaps between strands filled, the hood
  smooth), which CuMesh's `remesh_narrow_band_dc` does not expose; or a hair-specific smoothing of the
  rebuilt head. Not a quick fix; unscheduled.
- **The knight's visor** (found with M24): the dark eye slits and the shadowed gap under the visor became
  deep black sockets at generation; `clean` with `hole` 0.10 leaves them (recesses, not holes). Same
  family: a thin dark feature read as empty space. `docs/bench/m24.md`.
- **The hiker's fingers.** Already mitten-and-spike at generation (Pixal3D at 1024: thin pointed fingers
  fused at the base); clean keeps them; the 15k decimation turns them into stubs. The lever is the face
  budget on thin parts (CuMesh `simplify` takes no importance weights here, so protecting hands would be
  a region-wise two-pass decimation) and, above that, the generator's resolution (the 1536 profile in
  PLAN). A 15k character cannot hold five fingers per hand; the owner decides between a higher budget
  for characters and mitten hands.

What changed: `bake` fills a texel whose sample finds no volume at all (alpha 0: the empty gap of a
double shell, a pit between strands) from its neighbours like a gutter, instead of baking black; the
alpha channel keeps the sample. It did not change the kid's dent (that one samples the scalp, not
emptiness) and is kept as a robustness fix with a test. The kid's experiment versions `300_bake`,
`310_clean`, `320_remesh` are left in the library for the owner to look at.
