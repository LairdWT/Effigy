# M41e: the snake

`python -m tools.creature_bench fox horse bird kaiju shark dragon spider snake --glb <cat>=fox --glb <test kaiju>=kaiju
--glb <test shark>=shark --glb <test dragon>=dragon --glb <test spider>=spider --glb <test snake>=snake`, as in `m41d.md`
(the `test` rows are copies of the owner's `test <id>` `005_clean`). Before = M41d (5d8ae4d), after = M41e. Inside is after
rig_fit's snap, score is Check fit's. The bench counts 27 snake joints: the root is left out (rig_fit counts it: 28).

What changed:

- **`steps/_templates.py` `_snake`**: a template with no limbs is placed along the mesh's own body: the longest path
  through the solid's core (voxels deeper than a third of the greatest depth, so loops lying close and merged by the
  voxels stay apart: the test snake's tail end nearly touches its loop and the whole-solid path short-cut across), from
  the deepest voxel the farthest, from that the farthest, through the middle of each step's ring. head, neck,
  tail01 … tail_tip at the template's distances along it; the head's own joints and the root turned to the head's
  heading (about the vertical, then lifted as the path is) and scaled by the length. `yaw` = the head's heading; a
  given `yaw` (the editor's Turn) picks the end heading nearer it. The surface is sampled densely (200000 points) for it:
  the sample's low-poly 30000 points left its fine voxels a hollow shell.
- **The head at the end whose middle stands higher**, not the thicker one as the spec says: the test snake's tail is
  cut off by the generator as thick as its body (mean depth over the last tenth 2.4 at both ends: a coin toss); its
  middle stands 7.3 voxels over the floor at the head, 5.2 at the tail (the sample: 14.2 against 11.3). A head is
  thicker and held up; a tail lies flat.
- **`level` stands a limbless template's flat mesh up too**: the test snake lies on a 21° slope (`leveled_deg` 21.2).
- `_steps` (the ring stepping, shared with `_limb`) and `_out` (the bone dict) split out of `_limb` and `fit`: same
  results (the table).
- **`blender_ops/creature_clips.py`**: with `headings` (anim passes it for a template with no limbs) each bone takes
  the template's turn about its own heading instead of the head's one `yaw`. The spec's check at build: on the curled
  test snake Bite lifted parts of the loop sideways (a lift about the head's sideways axis applied to bones heading
  elsewhere); now the front of the body rises and strikes as on the sample. A straight snake: the same as before.
- **`blender_ops/rig_qa.py`**: no left/right asymmetry measured without paired bones (the snake): a body curled in an
  S mirrors onto other parts of itself (the test snake 0.91, a fail). Reported as not measured (`null`, a warn).

Snake, sample: facing 4/4, 24/27 (rig_fit: 25/28; target 25/28), score 80 (target 45). Test snake: 25/27 (rig_fit 26/28;
target 24/28), score 74 (target 35). Outside: the template's jaw is open in its rest pose (`mouth`, `mouth_tip` hang

Skin QA (rig_skin's): sample no fail (symmetry warn: not measured). **Test snake: pose stretch fails (506)**: the
generator fused the snout to the body beside it (46 edges, 5-15 mm, between vertices weighted to `head_tip` and to
`tail16`); any move of the head against the body tears them (the thin line in the renders). A mesh issue, as the
test spider's touching legs (M41d): no weights fix it. All 7 clips (Bite, Coiled, Dance, Death, Hit, Idle, Side
winding) bake on both.

Single fit (`_templates.fit`, the machine otherwise idle): test snake 0.9 s (every joint outside) → 2.9 s. rig_fit on
the test snake 4.8 s.

Renders (`m41e_snake.jpg`): Side winding and Bite, four frames each, side and top; the test snake above, the sample
below. Sample: Side winding sends S waves along the flat body, Bite raises the neck like a cobra and strikes forward.
Test snake: Bite raises the front of the body upright and strikes, no sideways twist; Side winding adds the clip's
waves to the curl of the loop (the clip's turns about the vertical add to our curve, as the spec expected): the loop
opens and closes as it winds. The thin straight line is the fused snout's edges.
