# M31: parts, the spike (2026-10-07)

PLAN Phase 3 asks for part segmentation (rigid accessories as their own pieces, print splits). The
research notes named SegViGen; a survey on 2026-10-07 found it needs Linux, 24 GB VRAM, flash-attn,
nvdiffrast and nvdiffrec, so it is out, as are SAMPart3D (per-object training, flash-attn, spconv) and
SAMesh (no licence file). **GeoSAM2** (VAST-AI, CVPR 2026, Apache-2.0 code and checkpoint, 154 M
parameters, 616 MB on disk, built on Meta's SAM 2 under Apache-2.0) takes a click on one of twelve
rendered views and lifts the mask to per-face labels.

## What it took on this laptop

- A scratch venv with torch 2.8 cu128, its requirements and the package without its optional CUDA
  extension (`GEOSAM2_BUILD_CUDA=0`; the extension is a post-processing speed-up).
- OpenCV 4 (its EXR depth maps are read through `cv2.imread`; OpenCV 5 returns nothing).
- Its runtime C++ helper (a per-row mode, compiled at import with ninja and a compiler) replaced by a
  twelve-line torch function with the same rule.
- Its Blender render script is Blender-4-only (the compositor API changed in 5.0): the twelve views
  came from our own 60-line Blender 5.2 script with its conventions (longest side scaled to 1 and
  centred; a 50 mm lens on a 36 mm sensor at `50/36 × the bounding-box diagonal`; elevations 0/25/0/-25
  and azimuths every 30° from 180°; colour and world-space normals as RGBA WEBP with a transparent film;
  z-depth as a float32 EXR through Cycles at one sample, because EEVEE's film is half float and quantises
  depth to 1/1024, which its 1e-3 depth test does not forgive; `meta.json` with the camera-to-world
  matrices, the scale and the unscaled translation, which its loader applies before the scale).

## The viking

`m31_spike_views.jpg`: the twelve views. Three positive clicks on the helmet band and one negative on
the face in the front view (view 3):

| mode | time | result |
|---|---|---|
| single-view click → 2D mask | 5 s | the helmet band (2085 px) |
| 3D lift, prompt only, no post-processing | 9 s | label 1: 512 faces at the top of the head |
| 3D lift, prompt only, post-processed (`pa 0.02`) | 15 s | label 1: 887 faces, the helmet |
| 3D lift with the opposite view auto-segmented, post-processed | 66 s | helmet, head, shoulder pads, torso with arms, belt and strap, skirt, legs (`m31_spike_viking.jpg`) |

The renders take about 60 s per mesh (36 renders) and happen once per version. VRAM: a few hundred MB.

## Decision

GeoSAM2 is the engine for M31: a `parts` step (views once, then a click or the full decomposition),
`parts.json` with per-face labels and names, `parts.glb` with one node per part. The vendored pieces
(its `inference.py`, `utils/`, `configs/` are not in the installable package; the modified `sam2` is)
go under `engine/geosam2` with its LICENSE and NOTICE and a PATCHES.md row for the mode helper.

## M31b: the parts in the game files (2026-10-08)

The viking's `522_parts` (the full decomposition: Part 1–4 and Body) renamed through the API
(`parts` op `rename`, `523_parts`: Part 1 → "Torso and Arms", Part 2 → Legs, Part 3 → Skirt, Part 4 →
Belt; which piece is which from a render with one colour per part), then exported (`524_export` Godot,
`525_export` Unreal) and imported headless:

| engine | result |
|---|---|
| Godot 4.7.1 (`--headless --import`, the scene walked by a script) | five `MeshInstance3D` named Belt, Body, Legs, Skirt, Torso and Arms with the parts' triangle counts (377 / 5 205 / 1 750 / 921 / 7 148), 0.00–1.00 m tall, albedo + normal; a `StaticBody3D` with one `ConvexPolygonShape3D` per part (85–177 points); the bodies get generated names (`@StaticBody3D@…`): Godot strips `-convcolonly` and the name is taken by the mesh |
| Unreal 5.8 (`UnrealEditor-Cmd -run=pythonscript`, default `AssetImportTask`) | five static meshes Belt, Body, Legs, Skirt, Torso_and_Arms (Unreal turns the spaces into `_`), 4 LODs each, one convex hull each (from `UCX_<part>_00`), one shared material + the base colour and normal textures, heights in cm as in Godot |

Two findings that are not about parts, measured the same way on the unsplit viking (`090_bake`):

- **Unreal 5.8 turns Nanite on at import.** LOD0 then reads as Nanite's fallback mesh (the whole viking
  3 244 of 15 401 triangles; the parts 309 / 1 661 / 968 / 588 / 2 545). With Nanite switched off on the
  imported meshes LOD0 is the full part (377 / 5 205 / 1 750 / 921 / 7 148) and LOD1–3 are ours. A
  Nanite mesh renders without our LODs; untick Nanite at import to use them.
- **gltfpack's LODs stop at about a third on the viking**: with `-se 0.05` (5 % deviation) the whole
  mesh goes 15 401 → 7 699 → 5 223 → 5 225; the parts together the same (7 697 → 5 378 → 4 993). The
  M13 dwarf reached its 10 %; this mesh does not get there within 5 %. Raising `-se` is the knob if the
  far LODs matter.

Also run on `523_parts` into a scratch folder: OBJ (one `o` per part, one material), web (gltfpack with
`-kn`: the five names on the nodes, meshopt + WebP; USDZ beside it), Unity (the FBX as for Unreal) and
print (one solid STL of 15 401 faces until the print split). Rename: under a second, no GPU, the resident
worker kept.
