# Research 11: retexturing alternatives (2026-10-08)

> Four parallel research threads (dedicated 3D texturing models; PBR estimation and post-processing; DIY pipelines
> from commercial-clean 2D models; the Meshy/Tripo baseline and local tools), synthesized into this report. The
> per-candidate fact sheets with their sources are in `11-notes/`. Scope: a retexture step for a finished game mesh
> with its own UVs, text- or image-driven, more detail, proper PBR, masked edits; 16 GB, Windows, torch 2.8, sm_120,
> no nvdiffrast; the licence policy of PLAN §6. Partly supersedes §4b of [04-texturing](04-texturing.md).

# DIY Z-Image projection clears every hard constraint

As of October 2026 **no dedicated open 3D texturing model satisfies Effigy's five hard constraints at once** (commercial-clean code, weights and base; EU-usable; keeps the mesh's UVs; full PBR at 2K; runs in 16 GB on Windows without nvdiffrast), so the "render views → clean 2D model → back-project, fall back to TRELLIS.2 texels" design in `docs/research/04-texturing.md` is confirmed as the only viable route, but three of its component choices should change. Every 2026 model that produces 2K PBR (UltraTex, LumiTex, UniTEX) rides a FLUX.1-dev or FLUX.2-klein-9B base that is non-commercial, the strongest PBR painters (Hunyuan3D-Paint 2.1, MaterialMVP, Pandora3D) are Tencent-licensed with explicit EU exclusions, and the fully clean set (Material Anything, MV-Adapter, Paint3D, SyncMVD) is 2024–25 SD1.5/SD2.1/SDXL-era work that is either 1K, RGB-only, or chained to nvdiffrast, Kaolin or PyTorch3D. The 2D side has moved instead: **Z-Image-Turbo (Apache-2.0, 6B, 8 steps, "fits within 16G") now has an Apache depth/canny/inpaint/Tile ControlNet-Union**, Qwen-Image-Edit-2509/2511 (Apache, 20B, 4-bit on 16 GB) is the strongest clean masked editor, and the only peer-reviewed cross-view consistency method with better FID and fewer seams than grid or sequential projection, SyncMVD's UV-space latent synchronisation, is MIT and depends on nothing the project does not already own. The commercial bar is modest: Meshy and Tripo retexture in 1–2 minutes for roughly $0.10–0.30 at 2K with PBR and delighting, and both **re-unwrap the UVs by default**, so "UVs kept" is a differentiator rather than table stakes. Delighting, super-resolution and seam healing are solved best by targeted clean passes (StableDelight, HAT/SwinIR/AuraSR-v2, LaMa) on rendered views rather than a new intrinsic estimator, because the only clean intrinsic models are SD2-class and the frontier (Marigold V2 albedo, Apache) needs 17 GB at 1024². The recommendation is to build the Z-Image-Turbo + UV-latent-sync loop first, add masked edits with the same loop plus Qwen-Edit, keep TRELLIS.2/Pixal3D as PBR base and unseen-texel fallback, drop every FLUX-NC and Tencent candidate, and re-check UltraTex, Marigold V2 and the VAST/Tencent "native 3D" texturers on specific triggers listed at the end.

## No open texturer is clean, UV-safe, PBR, 2K and 16 GB at once

The dedicated-model survey covered roughly thirty candidates and sorted them by which hard constraint they fail. The nearest thing to a drop-in replacement is **Material Anything (MIT code, SD 2.1 backbone, estimator weights tagged Apache-2.0)**: it outputs albedo, roughness, metallic and bump through a per-view estimator plus a UV-space refiner, and runs xatlas only for meshes that lack UVs, but its maps are **1024²**, its renders are 512², and its dependency list needs PyTorch3D and Blender 3.2.2 on a Linux-style setup ([Material Anything README](https://github.com/3DTopia/MaterialAnything), [arXiv 2411.15138](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15138), [estimator weights](https://huggingface.co/xanderhuang/material_estimator)). Its FID of 100.63 on texture-less meshes is the best in its own table but that table's scale (Paint3D 153.20, SyncMVD 118.46) shows it is a 2024 result, and the paper does not state whether existing UVs survive when present ([arXiv 2411.15138](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15138)). **MV-Adapter** (Apache-2.0, ICCV 2025) is the only clean model that writes above 1K, a **4096 UV from six 768² SDXL views** with `uv_unwarp=False` by default so the loaded mesh keeps its UVs, but its texture pipeline hard-wires an `NVDiffRastContextWrapper` and a `cvcuda_cu12` requirement, three Windows issues (#51, #55, #58) stand open, and its image-geometry SDXL variant needs ">16 GB" (SD2.1 "<10 GB") ([MV-Adapter README](https://github.com/huanngzh/MV-Adapter), [pipeline_texture.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huanngzh/MV-Adapter/main/mvadapter/pipelines/pipeline_texture.py), [requirements.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huanngzh/MV-Adapter/main/requirements.txt), [Windows issues](https://github.com/huanngzh/MV-Adapter/issues?q=windows)). It is RGB-only. The remaining clean models are older still: Paint3D (Apache, 2K albedo, Kaolin 0.13 + PyTorch3D, code from April 2024), SyncMVD (MIT, SD1.5 + ControlNet depth/normalbae, PyTorch3D, honours existing `.obj/.mtl` UVs), TexPainter (MIT, 24 GB) and PBR-Boost (Apache, semi-automatic roughness/metal masks, nvdiffrast) ([Paint3D README](https://github.com/OpenTexture/Paint3D), [SyncMVD README](https://github.com/LIU-Yuxin/SyncMVD), [TexPainter README](https://github.com/Quantuman134/TexPainter), [PBR-Boost README](https://github.com/snowflakewang/PBR_Boost_3DGen)). None of them has dated activity in 2026.

The quality frontier is where the licence wall stands. **UltraTex** (SIGGRAPH Asia 2026, code and weights released 2026-10-03) generates six 2048² views trained on 4096² G-buffer renders and beats Hunyuan3D 2.1 on unshaded FID (**125.13 vs 153.48**) and LPIPS (0.082 vs 0.136), but its LoRAs exist only for FLUX.1-dev and FLUX.2-klein-9B, the README states the 9B LoRA "won't load into" the Apache klein-4B, and the weights carry no licence statement at all ([UltraTex README](https://github.com/yiboz2001/UltraTex), [arXiv 2609.23169](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23169), [FLUX.2-klein-9B licence](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-9B)). LumiTex (ICLR 2026) has an Apache repo but a FLUX.1-dev-derived base and Tencent Hunyuan3D-2.1 rendering code, and its own README warns both "are non-commercial and may restrict commercial use" ([LumiTex README](https://github.com/LumiTexPBR/LumiTex)). UniTEX fine-tunes two FLUX.1-dev DiTs and its Large Texturing Model checkpoint is still an open TODO ([UniTEX README](https://github.com/YixunLiang/UniTEX), [arXiv 2505.23253](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23253)). On the Tencent side, Hunyuan3D-Paint 2.1 needs **21 GB for texturing** and its licence territory is "the worldwide territory, excluding the territory of the European Union, United Kingdom and South Korea" ([Hunyuan3D-2.1 README](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1), [LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2.1/blob/main/LICENSE)); MaterialMVP's only released "professional" weights are those same Hunyuan3D-Paint-v2-1 weights ([MaterialMVP README](https://github.com/ZebinHe/MaterialMVP)); Pandora3D's "MIT with additional restrictions" prohibits use "within the European Union" ([Pandora3D README](https://github.com/Tencent/Tencent-XR-3DGen)); and Step1X-3D, Apache on paper, vendors a `custom_rasterizer` "adapted from Hunyuan3D 2.0" and needs 27 GB ([Step1X-3D README](https://github.com/stepfun-ai/Step1X-3D)). The 2026 "native 3D" texture generators that would sidestep multi-view projection altogether, NaTex, LaFiTe, PixTex, MV2UV and MatLat, are paper-only or unlicensed as of this month ([NaTex project page](https://natex-ldm.github.io/), [LaFiTe HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.04786), [PixTex ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3799902.3811120), [MV2UV arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15436)). TEXGen, SeqTex, TexGaussian and MVPaint ship no LICENSE file; TEXGen additionally re-unfolds every mesh with xatlas ([TEXGen README](https://github.com/cvmi-lab/TEXGen), [arXiv 2411.14740](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14740), [SeqTex README](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/SeqTex), [TexGaussian README](https://github.com/ymxbj/TexGaussian)).

One licence nuance, checked against the licence texts on 2026-10-08: **SD 2.x (Material Anything, MV-Adapter's SD2.1 variant) is under the same CreativeML Open RAIL++-M licence as SDXL** (the November 24, 2022 text; SDXL's is the July 26, 2023 text, and a sentence-level diff finds only the header and two wording changes, with an identical Attachment A), so it falls inside the policy's "OpenRAIL++" row as written. **SD 1.x (SyncMVD's and Paint3D's reference implementations) is the older CreativeML OpenRAIL-M** (August 22, 2022), which differs from RAIL++-M only in clause 7: the licensor also reserves the right to "update the Model through electronic means, or modify the Output of the Model based on updates" and asks for "reasonable efforts to use the latest version"; otherwise the grant (perpetual, royalty-free, any field of use), the "Licensor claims no rights in the Output" clause and the use restrictions are the same. Both model cards say "intended for research purposes only" under Direct Use, which is a model-card statement, not a licence term. Since the recommended path ports SyncMVD's method onto Z-Image-Turbo, no SD 1.x weights need to enter a preset; SD 2.x only would, and only for Material Anything. Note that the stabilityai SD 2.x repos answered anonymous reads with 401 during this check, so they may be gated behind an accepted-terms click now. The practical consequence of the whole matrix is that the pieces worth taking from this corner of the field are methods, not models: SyncMVD's synchronisation loop, Paint3D's "depth-inpaint sequential views, then UV-space inpaint of holes" ordering, Material Anything's "per-view estimate, UV-space refine" split, and MV-Adapter's SDXL geometry adapter as a fallback diffusion half in front of the project's own bake.

## Z-Image-Turbo plus UV-latent sync is the 16 GB engine

The 2D side offers what the 3D side does not. **Z-Image-Turbo** is Apache-2.0, 6B parameters, 8 function evaluations at `guidance_scale=0.0`, and its card says it "fits comfortably within 16G VRAM consumer devices"; diffusers runs it on SDPA by default with flash-attn only as an optional backend ([Z-Image-Turbo card](https://huggingface.co/Tongyi-MAI/Z-Image-Turbo)). Alibaba-PAI's **Z-Image-Turbo-Fun-Controlnet-Union 2.1** (Apache-2.0) adds canny, depth, pose, MLSD, HED, scribble and gray control, a separate **Tile model trained to 2048×2048** for super-resolution, an 8-step distilled variant, a **lite build of three control blocks at 1.9 GB**, and an **inpaint mode** driven by a larger `control_context_scale`; the recommended scale is 0.65–1.00 ([Fun-ControlNet-Union 2.1 card](https://huggingface.co/alibaba-pai/Z-Image-Turbo-Fun-Controlnet-Union-2.1)). It runs through the Apache-2.0 VideoX-Fun code, whose tested-environment table includes Windows 10 on torch 2.2 and an RTX 3060 12 GB, so the torch 2.8 / sm_120 combination is untested but the control transformer contains no custom CUDA op in the sources read ([VideoX-Fun README](https://github.com/aigc-apps/VideoX-Fun)). Two gaps matter: there is **no normal-map control** for Z-Image, and no core diffusers class yet, so the project would vendor the `ZImageControlPipeline` port from videox_fun or one of the diffusers-style community ports ([akhaliq Space app.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/akhaliq/Z-Image-Turbo-controlnet/blob/main/app.py), [fal FlashPack repack](https://huggingface.co/fal/Z-Image-Turbo-Control-2.1-FlashPack/blob/main/model_index.json)).

The other two Apache models in the pipeline are weaker as geometry-conditioned painters. **FLUX.2-klein-4B** has no official ControlNet or Control-LoRA at all; BFL's documented trick is to pass a depth map as a `reference_latents` image, and a node author notes klein "never partially denoises an input photo. Every edit starts from pure noise", so there is no img2img strength and no inpaint ([klein-4B card](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-4B), [FluxKleinControlNetImg2Img node notes](https://comfy.icu/node/FluxKleinControlNetImg2Img)). The one community adapter, **thedeoxen's refcontrol reference+depth LoRA for klein-base-4B**, is tagged Apache-2.0 on its HF card, takes a reference image and a depth map together, and is "trained mainly on humans" with a 50-step, guidance-4 recipe; the Civitai mirror carries FLUX.1-dev boilerplate that conflicts with the HF card, so the licence needs a second look before it ships in a preset ([refcontrol-4B depth LoRA](https://huggingface.co/thedeoxen/refcontrol-FLUX.2-klein-4B-reference-depth-lora), [Civitai mirror](https://civitai.com/models/2657241)). The author's canny, lineart, pose and normal LoRAs exist only for the non-commercial 9B ([author listing](https://huggingface.co/thedeoxen)). **Qwen-Image-Edit-2509** is Apache-2.0 with "Native Support for ControlNet: Including depth maps, edge maps, keypoint maps" via 1–3 concatenated input images, and **2511** adds a built-in new-viewpoint LoRA and reduced drift, but at 20B it is a 4-bit citizen on 16 GB (Q4 GGUF ~13.1 GB; the full BF16 pipeline needs ~20 GB), and Nunchaku ships `svdq-fp4` builds specifically for Blackwell ([Edit-2509 card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-Edit-2509), [Edit-2511 card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-Edit-2511), [Edit-2511 local specs](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/qwen-image-edit-2511-local-specs), [NF4 repo note](https://huggingface.co/libaya/Qwen-Image-Edit-2511-4bit)). Qwen-Image-2.1 moved to the Qwen Research License and is out ([Qwen-Image-2.1 card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1)). SDXL with xinsir's Apache ControlNet-Union remains the only clean stack with a **Normal** control and a ProMax inpaint/outpaint variant, at an 8 GB class, and is what StableGen's developer found more reliable than FLUX.1-dev on limited VRAM ([xinsir union card](https://huggingface.co/xinsir/controlnet-union-sdxl-1.0), [SDXL card](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0), [CGWorld on StableGen](https://cgworld.jp/flashnews/01-202511-StableGen.html)).

The consistency mechanism is where this research most directly changes the 04 design, which proposed "shared seeds or grid layout". Four families exist, and only one has peer-reviewed evidence of both fewer seams and better FID at low cost. **SyncMVD** denoises ten views at once, projects every step's predicted clean views into a shared UV latent texture, blends them with cosine-weighted visibility whose exponent rises over the schedule, fills gaps by Voronoi propagation, patches self-attention so each view attends to its neighbours, mirror and a reference view, and finishes the last steps unsynchronised; the paper reports **FID 50.26 vs TEXTure 74.36, Text2Tex 71.20, Paint3D 72.01 and Meshy 82.24**, wins the user study on natural colour (39.1 %), fewer artifacts (42.8 %) and 3D consistency (37.1 %), and takes **60–150 s per mesh** on SD1.5 ([SyncMVD paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.12891v2)). An independent 2025 comparison (FlexPainter) confirms the ordering: SyncMVD FID 73.4 / KID 41.7 against TEXTure 78.3 / 107.1, Text2Tex 89.0 / 94.4 and Paint3D 77.8 / 123.6, with the best KID of every method tested, and its related-work section names the failure modes of the alternatives: sequential per-view generation lets "inconsistencies arise easily", and multi-view grid generation suffers "ghosting" after reprojection because of misalignment, with brittle heuristic weight maps ([FlexPainter](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02620v1)). GOATex (NeurIPS 2025) builds on the same module with sixteen views at 768 px in **~12 GB on an A6000** and adds ray-cast "hit levels" to texture occluded layers separately, but publishes no code ([GOATex](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23051v1)). The method is model-agnostic in principle, since it operates on predicted x0 latents, and it needs exactly two primitives the project already has: forward rasterisation of per-view UV/visibility maps and a depth-tested, cosine-weighted back-projection into UV space. Porting it means replacing PyTorch3D with `uvraster`, re-deriving the x0 hook for flow matching (x0 = x_t − σ·v) on Z-Image's 16-channel latent, and installing the attention patch through diffusers' attention-processor API; nothing in SyncMVD needs nvdiffrast, Kaolin, xformers or Linux once PyTorch3D is gone.

VRAM and time have to be estimated, because no source measures any of this on a 16 GB or Blackwell card, on Windows, or on a DiT base. The anchors are: Z-Image-Turbo's 6B bf16 weights are roughly 12 GB, so the full 15-block union will not fit alongside the resident Qwen3 text encoder; the realistic configuration is the **1.9 GB lite union with prompts pre-encoded and the text encoder offloaded**. Synchronised denoising batches all views per step, and SyncMVD's ten views at 96×96 latents already needs the batch resident, so on 16 GB the DiT forwards must be chunked per view: 8 steps × 8 views becomes 64 sequential forwards plus 8 UV sync rounds, which the notes place at **about 1–3 minutes per asset at 1024 px views**, with a per-view Tile-SR refine to 2048 px adding roughly the same again. Synchronised 2048 px for all views at once is not affordable; 2048 is a per-view refine pass with the previous pass projected as context. For comparison, the documented points on bigger cards are TEXGen under 10 s on an A100, TexGaussian ~21 s, Step1X-3D 152 s at 27 GB, and StableProjectorz "most generation runs finish in 2–5 minutes" on an RTX 4090 ([arXiv 2411.14740](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14740), [TexGaussian arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19654v1), [Step1X-3D README](https://github.com/stepfun-ai/Step1X-3D), [superrendersfarm on StableProjectorz](https://superrendersfarm.com/article/stable-projectorz-3d-texture-generation-ai)).

For the **new-reference-image** case the research changes little, because the project already owns the best clean tool: TRELLIS.2's shape-conditioned texturing pipeline exports base color, roughness, metallic and opacity at `texture_size=4096`, and **Pixal3D shipped multi-view inference in September 2026** (`inference_mv.py`, separate MV weights, front view first, two views default), which is the multi-image retexture path TRELLIS.2 upstream never delivered; neither has text or style conditioning ([TRELLIS.2 repo](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2), [Pixal3D repo](https://github.com/TencentARC/Pixal3D)). The 2D loop earns its place for the reference case only when the reference must be followed at higher fidelity than the voxel field allows, where the refcontrol-4B LoRA (reference + depth in one adapter) or Z-Image with an IP-Adapter-style style path is the candidate, with the 50-step base-model recipe costing roughly twelve times the compute of distilled klein.

## PBR, detail and seams come from targeted passes, not a new estimator

The PBR survey's headline is negative in a useful way: **no delighting or PBR-estimation model is both commercial-clean and documented at the ≤4 GB the step budget assumed**; the clean ones are all SD2-class UNets of 0.9–1.3B parameters that run at 512–768 px and must be tiled to reach 2K. Marigold-IID v1-1 (OpenRAIL++) resizes the longer side to 768 px, was trained on InteriorVerse at 480×640, and scores PSNR 19.50 on InteriorVerse albedo against 18.10 for Kocsis' IID-Diffusion and 13.16 for RGB↔X ([Marigold-IID card](https://huggingface.co/prs-eth/marigold-iid-appearance-v1-1), [Marigold journal paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09358)); StableGen's own release notes label its albedo "flat" and the IID-Lighting variant "vibrant", which matches what the owner already observed ([StableGen releases](https://github.com/sakalond/stablegen/releases)). **StableDelight** (Apache-2.0, one-step, Stable-X) removes specular reflections only; it was trained on specular-highlight datasets and makes no claim about diffuse shading, shadows or AO ([StableDelight README](https://github.com/Stable-X/StableDelight), [yoso-delight card](https://huggingface.co/Stable-X/yoso-delight-v0-4-base)). The better-measured 2026 albedo models are all excluded: albedoLBM is CC BY-NC 4.0, RGB↔X is under the Adobe Research License ("noncommercial research purposes only"), NVIDIA's SVD DiffusionRenderer and UniRelight are under the Nvidia Source Code License, and the Cosmos variant, whose weights are under the allowed NVIDIA Open Model License, installs nvdiffrast from GitHub, downloads 56 GB and needs 16–48 GB ([albedoLBM README](https://github.com/CVC-Color/albedoLBM), [RGB↔X LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zheng95z/rgbx/main/LICENSE), [Cosmos-DiffusionRenderer README](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/cosmos1-diffusion-renderer)). The only post-2025 clean leap is **Marigold V2** (Apache-2.0, 2026-09-08), which swaps the backbone for a frozen Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 DiT quantised to 4-bit on load and predicts depth, normals and linear albedo in one step, but it needs **~17 GB at 1024² and ~29 GB at 2048²**, and the README is Linux-only; ComfyUI ships an int8 base with per-task LoRAs, which is the only hint that a 16 GB run exists ([Marigold V2 README](https://github.com/huawei-bayerlab/marigold-v2), [ComfyUI Marigold V2 docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/utility/marigold-v2)).

The direct evidence that a second delighting pass improves a generator's albedo is absent; what the 3D literature does report is that distilled albedo "suffers from incorrect material decomposition, such as baked-in shading effects" ([DreamMat](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.17176v1)), and the survey notes projection methods "can bake specularities and shadows into albedo" ([2026 texturing survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00137v1)). Because the voxel bake already approximates albedo, a full intrinsic re-estimation at 768 px effective resolution is more likely to flatten detail than to add it. The defensible recipe is therefore surgical: keep TRELLIS.2's metallic and roughness, run StableDelight on rendered views only where the bake shows baked highlights (faces, metal), divide out the Blender AO bake where crevices are darkened (no model needed), and prompt the 2D loop for "flat lighting / albedo / no shadows" so less needs removing in the first place. Every intrinsic model surveyed is trained on photographs or rendered scene views, none on atlases, and the UV-inpainting literature states that "any inpainting method applied to the global texture would produce visible seam artefacts" unless patches are reparametrised per island, so every one of these passes must run on views and go back through the UV rasteriser, not on the atlas ([Maggiordomo et al. 2023](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14735)).

For "more detail than the voxel-limited 2K bake", the research separates two sources. Detail above the voxel ceiling comes from view resolution, not UV size: MV-Adapter's 4096 UV carries about 768 px of real detail per view, and the only open weights whose 2048² views plausibly resolve fabric weave or lettering are UltraTex's non-commercial ones. On a 16 GB card the affordable path is the Z-Image **Tile model (trained to 2048×2048)** as a per-view refine, followed by a clean super-resolver. Several Apache alternatives to Real-ESRGAN exist and all load through spandrel (MIT, pure torch, tile mode): HAT (Apache-2.0, Real_HAT_GAN_SRx4 with a "sharper" variant), SwinIR, and fal's **AuraSR-v2 (Apache-2.0, 0.6B, GigaGAN-derived)**, which fal tuned for "AI-generated or high-quality uncompressed images", the exact character of a TRELLIS bake ([HAT README](https://github.com/XPixelGroup/HAT), [AuraSR-v2 card](https://huggingface.co/fal/AuraSR-v2), [fal AuraSR-v2 blog](https://blog.fal.ai/aurasr-v2), [spandrel README](https://github.com/chaiNNer-org/spandrel)). Replicate's side-by-side found "SwinIR has much better textures, particularly in the facial hair and skin detail" than Real-ESRGAN at 4 s versus 1.4 s, with the caveat that it "doesn't handle eyes well" ([Replicate SwinIR guide](https://replicate.ai/guides/upscaling-images/swinir)); a 2022 paper found Real-ESRGAN "generates unnatural textures" ([HST arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.09885)). Diffusion SR on SD 2.1 (OSEDiff one-step, SeeSR, DiffBIR, all Apache-2.0) hallucinates pores and weave for photo-real faces at 6–10 GB with tiling ([OSEDiff README](https://github.com/cswry/OSEDiff), [SeeSR README](https://github.com/cswry/SeeSR), [DiffBIR README](https://github.com/XPixelGroup/DiffBIR)); SUPIR and InvSR are non-commercial ([SUPIR README](https://github.com/Fanghua-Yu/SUPIR), [InvSR README](https://github.com/zsyOAOA/InvSR)). No independent benchmark of any of these on game textures was found, so this is a bench item for the owner's golden set, not a settled ranking. For albedo-derived micro-normal detail the only tool that works on the atlas directly is DeepBump (GPL-3.0, ONNX, CPU-capable), which fits the project's `blender_ops/` GPL boundary as a separate process; the view-space normal estimators (StableNormal, Lotus, Marigold normals, all Apache) only matter if the mesh normals are poor, which a 1024³ voxel field makes unlikely ([DeepBump README](https://github.com/HugoTini/DeepBump), [StableNormal README](https://github.com/Stable-X/StableNormal)).

Seams and gutters have a clean deterministic answer. **LaMa** is Apache-2.0 end to end (code and big-lama weights), "generalizes surprisingly well" from 256² training to about 2K, loads through spandrel, and is CNN/FFC pure torch; MAT, the obvious alternative, is CC BY-NC 4.0 ([LaMa LICENSE](https://github.com/advimman/lama/blob/main/LICENSE), [LaMa README](https://github.com/advimman/lama), [MAT LICENSE](https://github.com/fenglinglwb/MAT/blob/main/LICENSE)). The cheap path is the one the bake already starts: dilate and pad islands (Meshy's own guidance is ≥4 px at 1K and 8 px at 2K), then LaMa-inpaint only the gutters and masked defects per island ([Meshy texture generator page](https://www.meshy.ai/features/ai-texture-generator)). For props that need a tileable material rather than a mesh-specific texture, no clean faithful photo-to-SVBRDF estimator exists in the open (MatE has no code, Chord is under Ubisoft's research-only licence), but **StableMaterials** (OpenRAIL, 512², `tileable=True` via feature rolling, image prompts) and **MatFuse** (MIT, ≥12 GB, CLIP-image conditioning) generate tileable basecolor/normal/height/roughness/metallic sets, and MatSynth's 4,000+ CC0 materials are the cleanest training set if the owner ever fine-tunes ([StableMaterials card](https://huggingface.co/gvecchio/StableMaterials), [MatFuse README](https://github.com/giuvecchio/matfuse-sd), [MatE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18312v1), [Chord README](https://github.com/ubisoft/ubisoft-laforge-chord), [MatSynth](https://research.adobe.com/publication/matsynth-a-modern-pbr-materials-dataset/)).

## Masked edits and kept UVs are where a local tool beats Meshy

The commercial baseline is lower than the marketing suggests. Meshy's retexture API takes a mesh plus exactly one of a text style prompt, a style image, or (Meshy-7) one to four multi-view images; `enable_pbr` defaults to false, `texture_resolution` defaults to 2K with 4K/8K paid, `remove_lighting` defaults to true, and **`enable_original_uv` defaults to false**, with the docs recommending it be left false for third-party meshes "so Meshy can unwrap fresh UVs" ([Meshy Retexture API](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/api/retexture)). A 2K retexture takes about a minute for 10 credits and an 8K one about two minutes for 15 ([Meshy PBR tutorial](https://www.meshy.ai/tutorials/pbr-texturing-with-meshy)); at a third-party-listed $20 for 1,000 credits that is roughly $0.20–0.30 per run ([Meshy API pricing](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/api/pricing), [fitgap listing](https://us.fitgap.com/products/045174/meshy)). Tripo's texture endpoint defaults `pbr` and `delight` to true, offers fast/standard/detailed/extreme tiers up to 8K, accepts a `style_image` beside text or exactly four orthogonal images, exposes `part_names` from a prior segmentation to texture only chosen parts, and **documents no UV option at all**; a credit is $0.01 and texturing costs 10–30 credits ([Tripo texture endpoint](https://developers.tripo3d.com/en/docs/models-texture.md), [Tripo pricing](https://developers.tripo3d.ai/en/pricing)). Rodin's texture-only endpoint requires a UV'd mesh, outputs PBR or shaded at 2K/4K from 0.5 credit, and says nothing about keeping the layout ([Hyper3D API page](https://hyper3d.ai/features/api)). The vendors' own troubleshooting pages list the failure modes of multi-view projection, seams at UV islands, smearing where the projection does not follow geometry, soft faces on fast tiers, baked lighting, and their answers are delighting toggles, healing brushes, "fix the mesh and UVs first", and hand touch-up in Substance Painter ([Meshy texture generator page](https://www.meshy.ai/features/ai-texture-generator), [Tripo failure modes](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/common-failure-modes-of-ai-3d-generators-and-fixes)). A Meshy designer's own 8K write-up concludes "Resolution is the price of entry; de-lighting is the competition" and "If 4K survives your closest shot, don't pay for 8K" ([HackerNoon Meshy 8K](https://hackernoon.com/i-ran-the-same-models-through-meshy-8k-ai-texturing-resolution-detail-and-de-lighting)). The target spec that falls out of this and the art-direction guides is **2K per material by default, 4K opt-in for hero faces and props, Base Color (sRGB, delighted) + Normal (OpenGL, tangent space) + Roughness + Metallic (linear, near-binary) with an ORM pack, ≥8 px padding at 2K**, and no 8K ([Strayspark resolution guide](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/texture-resolution-guide-games-512-1k-2k-4k)).

Masked local editing is now expected (Meshy Texture Edit, Tripo Magic Brush), but only as "orbit to the angle, draw a mask, type a prompt, preview, commit", with a separate healing brush for seams and undo/redo; per-material prompts exist only as Tripo's `part_names`, and per-view accept/reject exists nowhere ([Meshy Texture Edit guide](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/guides/texture-edit), [Tripo Magic Brush](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/repaint-part-of-3d-texture)). No open project does instruction-based, mask-local, multi-view-consistent editing end to end, but the recipe used by the two local tools is fully described and uses only primitives the project owns. StableGen's "Local Edit" blends the new texture over the old with angle-based and silhouette feathering, its "UV Inpaint" fills untextured UV regions from surrounding texture, and Qwen Image Edit is wired into Local Edit with Lightning-LoRA and Nunchaku Int4 options; StableProjectorz limits projection to inpaint-mask areas, uses "WhereEmpty" masks to paint only untextured surfaces, dilates 5 px for seams and preserves the original UVs and UDIMs ([StableGen README](https://github.com/sakalond/StableGen), [stableprojectorz.com](https://stableprojectorz.com/), [StableProjectorz repo](https://github.com/IgorAherne/StableProjectorz)). Both are copyleft (GPL-3.0 and AGPL-3.0 since January 2026), so the method can be reimplemented but no code vendored into the MIT core. The specific 2D risk is documented by TexEditor (March 2026): state-of-the-art editors "tend to regenerate the edited subject" instead of changing only its texture, and a Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 fine-tune with a structure reward raised structure retention from **0.642 to 0.929** on its benchmark, but its licence is unstated ([TexEditor](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18488v1)). The cheaper substitute is to pass the rendered depth or edge map as a second input image, which Edit-2509 documents natively. Re-expressed on `uvraster`, the edit path is: pick the one to three cameras that best see the masked faces (per-face visibility is already computed), render current albedo and depth, edit with Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 at 4-bit with depth pinned, or more cheaply inpaint with the Z-Image union in inpaint mode using the rendered RGB as context, back-project only inside mask × visibility × cosⁿ, run a second sequential pass from new cameras for masked faces no camera saw, and LaMa the residue in UV space. Because a game mesh already has material slots, "per-material prompt" is just mask = material slot, which the headless Blender ops already know and which no commercial tool offers.

The ready-made local tools confirm the hardware fit but are not integration targets. StableGen runs on Windows with SDXL at ≥8 GB and klein at ~13 GB through a ComfyUI backend and bakes to existing UVs if Smart UV Project is skipped, but it is an interactive add-on for Blender 4.2–4.5 or 5.1+ and its TRELLIS.2 "Native" mode pulls in nvdiffrast ([StableGen README](https://github.com/sakalond/StableGen)); StableProjectorz is a Unity GUI on Forge; Dream Textures has no 2025/2026 release and targets Blender 4.1 ([Dream Textures releases](https://github.com/carson-katri/dream-textures/releases)); ComfyUI-3D-Pack (MIT) bakes multi-view images "using nvdiffrast" ([ComfyUI-3D-Pack README](https://github.com/MrForExample/ComfyUI-3D-Pack)); ArmorPaint 1.0 (zlib, September 2026) is a painter with local neural upscale and photo-to-material nodes, not a mesh-aware retexturer ([CG Channel on ArmorPaint 1.0](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=178507)). Blender add-ons in this space are GPL because the extensions platform requires GPL-3.0-or-later, which is precisely why the project's split of an MIT torch core and a GPL `blender_ops/` process is the right shape for reusing their ideas ([Blender extension licences](https://docs.blender.org/manual/ja/4.2/advanced/extensions/licenses.html)).

Re-check later, each on a concrete trigger. **UltraTex**: a FLUX.2-klein-4B LoRA branch or an explicit weights licence; it is the only open 2K-view texturer with a Windows-friendly dependency list (Python 3.12, Triton only) and would slot straight in front of the existing bake. **Marigold V2 albedo**: any documented run under 16 GB at 1024² (int8/NF4), because it is the one clean intrinsic model with a modern backbone and would decide whether a delight pass is worth it. **NaTex, LaFiTe, PixTex, MV2UV, MatLat**: a code or weights drop with a licence file, since native-3D or direct-UV generation would remove the projection loop's seam and Janus problems. **Z-Image-Edit / Z-Image-Omni-Base**: public weights ("To be released" on the card), which would give an Apache editor in the same 6B family as the painter. **Z-Image normal control**: any Apache normal ControlNet for Z-Image or klein-4B, which would let the Blender normal render drive generation directly. **Pixal3D or TRELLIS.2 text/style conditioning upstream**, which would make the voxel path text-driven without the 2D loop. **TexEditor** weights and licence, since a structure-preserving Qwen-Edit fine-tune is the right model for masked edits. **MV-Adapter Windows support** (issues #51/#55/#58) and a core diffusers `ZImageControlPipeline`, either of which lowers porting cost. **A clean photo-to-tileable-SVBRDF estimator** (MatE or Chord re-licensed, or a MatSynth-trained release) for props. **Qwen-Image-Edit** licence drift: 2509 and 2511 are Apache today, and 2.1's move to a research licence shows the family can change, so pin the exact revisions.

Against the 04 design, this research **confirms** the architecture (render views, clean 2D model with ControlNet, depth-tested back-projection, TRELLIS.2 texels where unseen, TRELLIS.2 as PBR base), confirms keeping TRELLIS.2 metallic/roughness, confirms the text → image → TRELLIS.2 texturing route as the cheapest text path to hole-free full PBR, confirms the Hunyuan exclusion and MV-Adapter's status as a dep-swapped reference design, and confirms that SR must run on views or per-chart, never on the whole atlas. It **changes** four things: the consistency mechanism from "shared seeds or grid layout" to UV-space latent synchronisation, because grid ghosts after reprojection and sequential drifts while sync has the FID and user-study evidence; the first-choice 2D model from an unranked list to Z-Image-Turbo with the Fun union (the only Apache stack with depth, inpaint and a 2K Tile model in one package), with SDXL + xinsir as the normal-control fallback and Qwen-Edit reserved for masked edits; the "re-estimate with Marigold-IID" option to a targeted StableDelight-plus-AO-divide pass, because Marigold-IID is 768 px and flat and no evidence shows a full re-estimation helps a generator's albedo; and the claim that "no inpainting model is needed", which holds for the full retexture of a mesh with a voxel field but not for masked edits and gutters, where LaMa (Apache) is the cheap addition. It **extends** the design with the masked-edit recipe and the per-material-slot prompt, the target spec (2K default, 4K hero, ORM, OpenGL normals, ≥8 px padding, no 8K), the clean SR shortlist to benchmark against Real-ESRGAN, the Pixal3D multi-view path for the reference case, DeepBump in the GPL process for micro-normals, StableMaterials/MatFuse for props, and the trigger list above.

## Conclusion

The field split in 2025–26 along a licence seam that happens to run exactly where Effigy needs to be: the research groups with rights to 2K PBR training data (TexVerse, Objaverse-XL) built on FLUX or Hunyuan bases that cannot ship in an EU commercial game, while the Apache ecosystem invested in 2D control and editing (Z-Image union, Qwen-Edit, klein multi-reference). That means the project's competitive position is not "catch up to UltraTex" but "own the projection loop", where the valuable intellectual property is the synchronisation and blending maths that SyncMVD and GOATex published under MIT and CC-BY-NC paper licences respectively, and the hardware constraint (chunked per-view forwards under 16 GB) is a scheduling problem rather than a model problem. The same seam explains why "UVs kept" is cheap for this project and absent from Meshy and Tripo: commercial tools re-unwrap because their painters were trained on their own atlases, whereas a projection loop is indifferent to the layout.

Two implications follow for sequencing. First, the loop's cost is dominated by DiT forwards, not by the bake, so the owner's bench should measure seconds per view on the 5080 before deciding view count and resolution; if Z-Image-Turbo lands near its card's sub-second H800 figure scaled to a laptop, eight 1024 px views plus a 2048 px Tile refine is a 2–5 minute job that beats the commercial baseline on UVs and matches it on time, and if it does not, SDXL at 8 GB is the fallback that costs one adapter swap. Second, every watch-list trigger is a single file appearing (a klein-4B LoRA, a LICENSE, a 16 GB Marigold V2 run), so the right cadence is a quarterly check of six repositories rather than another survey; the next structural change worth a survey is a clean native-3D texture generator with a licence file, which none of the 2026 papers has yet produced.
